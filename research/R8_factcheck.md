## 2. Strongest Counter-Positions Pre-Empted (IDA Principle)

1. **Counter-Position 1: "The Sea Peoples are an Egyptian Invention to Cover Up Civil War"**
   * *Strongest Objection:* Pharaohs regularly exaggerated victories. Maybe the Sea Peoples were minor pirate bands and Egypt concocted an invasion myth.
   * *Audit Verification:* The widespread destruction of cities outside of Egypt (Ugarit, Enkomi, Hattusa, Mycenae, Hazor) is physical archaeological fact, confirmed by burnt destruction layers dating precisely to c. 1200–1175 BCE. Ramesses III was defending against an existing regional contagion.
   * *Script Protection:* Egypt's self-aggrandizing temple inscriptions are treated with proper historiographical attribution (`[ATTRIBUTED]`), not unquestioned fact.
2. **Counter-Position 2: "The Ugarit Scribes Baked the Tablet During the Battle"**
   * *Strongest Objection:* A popular YouTube cliché claims scribes frantically placed RS 20.238 into a kiln as invaders stormed the gate.
   * *Audit Verification:* Debunked by Itamar Singer. The kiln was a bread oven built by later squatters. The tablet fell from an upper floor.
   * *Script Protection:* The script explicitly mentions the popular legend and provides the updated archaeological correction.
3. **Counter-Position 3: "Iron Weapons Gave the Barbarians Superiority Over Bronze"**
   * *Strongest Objection:* Old schoolbooks claimed invaders with superior iron weapons easily cut through soft bronze armor.
   * *Audit Verification:* Iron weapons did not become widespread or superior until *after* the collapse. Early iron was brittle and inferior to well-tempered tin bronze. Iron was adopted out of pure desperation because tin trade routes had collapsed.
   * *Script Protection:* The script explicitly explains iron as an accidental byproduct of supply-chain starvation, not an invader advantage.
